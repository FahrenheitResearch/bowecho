# BowEcho v0.29.1 — Eyes on the Storm

v0.29.1 takes BowEcho beyond the radar dome and out to the whole planet:
live tropical cyclones from every ocean basin, satellite infrared in full
color, and a single-radar hurricane wind estimator — landing while a real
Category-5 super typhoon sits on the Guam radar.

## Tropical cyclones

A new **Tropical cyclones** toggle in Settings (off by default) turns on a
live, worldwide tropical-storm layer. A storm-card panel lists every active
tropical cyclone on Earth, strongest first — West Pacific super typhoons,
Indian Ocean and Southern-Hemisphere cyclones, and Atlantic/Pacific
hurricanes together, not just the US basins.

- Each card carries a basin-aware classification (e.g. "Category 4
  Hurricane," "Super Typhoon"), max wind in three units (kt / mph / km/h),
  minimum pressure, motion, threatened land areas, a color-coded
  Red/Orange/Green alert badge, and the data source with how long ago it
  updated.
- A **Focus** button recenters the map on that storm; one-click links open
  the official NHC advisory, Zoom Earth, Tropical Tidbits, and CyclonicWx.
- On the map, each storm draws a colored intensity dot with its name; where
  geometry is available it also draws the forecast track and cone of
  uncertainty.

It resolved Super Typhoon BAVI-26 near Guam in real time.

*Known limitations:* track and cone currently draw for GDACS-sourced storms
(West Pacific, Indian Ocean, Southern Hemisphere); NHC storms (Atlantic,
East/Central Pacific) show a position dot and name for now, with track/cone
geometry planned. GDACS cards omit pressure and motion (the feed does not
carry them). The list refreshes every 10 minutes. All tropical feeds used
here are free and keyless.

## Colorized satellite IR

Satellite infrared is no longer black-and-white. Cold cloud and storm tops
now light up green → yellow → orange → red → dark with magenta/white
overshoot tips, while warm surface stays gray — the classic enhanced-IR look
for tropical and severe storms. **Himawari (h9) IR, which used to render in
grayscale, is now in color** — on a real full-disk frame you can make out the
eye of Super Typhoon BAVI-26.

Coloring applies to the longwave-window IR bands (GOES ABI 13/14/15 and
Himawari AHI IR). On the radar map overlay, warm cloud and surface fade to
transparent so radar and the basemap show through while cold storm tops stay
opaque; the full-screen satellite player stays fully opaque. Water-vapor and
visible imagery are unchanged.

*Known limitation:* GOES 13–15 uses a fixed, temperature-anchored color
scale. Himawari's brightness-temperature bands are not stored in true Kelvin,
so Himawari uses a per-frame auto-stretch — its colors are relative contrast
per frame, not tied to an absolute cloud-top temperature.

## TC Winds (GBVTD)

With a radar volume loaded over a hurricane, pick the new **TC Winds (GBVTD)**
product and click the storm's eye — BowEcho estimates the storm's circulation
from that single radar's velocity data using the ground-based velocity track
display method (Lee, Jou, Chang & Deng 1999; simplex center after Lee & Marks
2000).

- It draws the retrieved center (red crosshair), the radius of maximum wind
  (yellow ring), and a "TC ## kt" label, in both single- and multi-pane
  views.
- A side panel reports peak wind (m/s and kt), the RMW in km, the beam tilt
  used, a Saffir–Simpson category, and a table of the axisymmetric tangential
  (VT) and radial (VR) wind at each radius ring.
- A magenta dot marks exactly where you clicked and the crosshair shows where
  the algorithm snapped the center, so you can watch it lock onto the
  eyewall. **Re-run at current center** recomputes on a new frame without
  re-clicking.

On the KLIX Hurricane Ida landfall volume it returns a sane ~10 km RMW and
~78 m/s Category-4 eyewall.

*Known limitations:* this is the GBVTD axisymmetric (wavenumber-0) core — mean
tangential/radial wind per ring — not the full asymmetric retrieval or
environmental-wind removal. The result is sensitive to where you click: the
center search spans only ±32 km around the click, so click on or near the
eye. It runs on the lowest velocity elevation cut, needs velocity data and a
known radar-site location, and click-to-place is primary-pane only.

## Fixes

- **Himawari IR rendered solid black.** An initial colorization pass assumed
  Himawari values were Kelvin and clamped the whole disk to the dark end.
  Himawari now auto-stretches and colorizes correctly.
- **Tangled tropical-cyclone track.** BAVI-26's track drew as several
  overlapping paths with spurious cross-map connectors; track segments are
  now kept separate and each drawn on its own polyline.
- **Alaska SPS map lag.** An extremely detailed coastline-traced hazard
  polygon (an Alaska marine Special Weather Statement, or a large
  multi-county watch union with thousands of vertices) could drop the whole
  map to ~1 FPS. The map now stays smooth. For those very complex polygons
  only, the translucent interior shading is skipped; the outline and label
  still draw exactly as before, in the correct place. Every normal warning
  and watch is unchanged — outline and fill both still render.

## Under the hood

Dependency bump to rusty-weather v0.2.3. This is an engine update with no
user-facing change in this release — the upstream v0.2.3 features (custom
plot domains, GUI/WRF soundings, editable color tables) are not wired into
BowEcho's UI yet; that is the next release.
